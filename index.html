City Walk, Dubai · Residents' guide · October 2026

Water dripping from your ceiling? Here's how to get it fixed.

Since summer 2026, chilled water pipes above ceilings in at least 11 apartments across 7 City Walk buildings have been "sweating". The water soaks the ceiling, and some homes now have mould.

This guide walks you through measuring the problem with a cheap gadget, understanding it, and sending the right letters in the right order until it's fixed. You don't need any technical background.

It works. In Building 9 the building's fresh air supply was adjusted in early October 2026 after this process, and the condensation stopped.

1Is this your problem?

You probably have it if two or more of these are true:

  • Water drips from a ceiling, a light fitting or an access hatch, often worst early in the morning.
  • A brown or yellow stain, bubbling paint or soft plasterboard has appeared on the ceiling.
  • You can see droplets on the pipe insulation above the ceiling, or the insulation feels wet.
  • Dark spots or a musty smell (mould) near the stain.
  • It started in summer 2026, and nothing in your apartment changed.
  • It gets better when you run your air conditioning hard and comes back when you switch it off or travel.

2Why it happens, in plain words

Above your ceiling, two pipes bring the building's chilled water, at about 5–7 °C, from the riser outside your apartment to your fan coil unit (your AC). They are wrapped in aluminium-covered insulation and carry green tags: CHWS (supply) and CHWR (return). The insulation keeps the outside surface at around 20–21 °C. That is fine, as long as the air around the pipes is dry enough.

That air comes from the building. A duct marked FA (fresh air) releases treated fresh air straight into your ceiling void, not into your room. So the condition of that fresh air decides whether the pipes stay dry.

What is "dewpoint"?

Air always carries some invisible water vapour. The dewpoint is the temperature at which that vapour turns back into liquid water. Think of a cold can from the fridge on a humid day: it gets wet because its surface is colder than the air's dewpoint.

The rule that matters for you is simple:

If the pipe's surface is colder than the dewpoint of the air around it, water forms on the pipe. Always. Better insulation, a new ceiling or a repaint won't help if the air stays this wet.

Why the void temperature alone tells you nothing

Temperature and dewpoint are different things. Two voids can both be at 26 °C, but:

  • at 50% humidity the dewpoint is about 15 °C. That is well below the 20 °C pipe surface, so the pipe stays dry;
  • at 75% humidity the dewpoint is about 21 °C. That is above the pipe surface, so the pipe drips.

Same temperature, opposite result. That's why a technician who only checks temperature, or tells you the fresh air "is supplied at 22–23 °C, which is sufficient", has not checked the thing that actually causes the water. You need temperature and humidity, and from those the dewpoint.

4Measure and record

A. The fresh air arriving in your ceiling void

  1. Open the ceiling access hatch nearest your AC unit and find the duct marked FA. Its open end blows the building's fresh air into the void.
  2. Switch your AC off for 15 minutes, so your fan coil unit doesn't disturb the reading. The FA duct keeps blowing either way.
  3. Hold the meter 5–10 cm from the FA duct outlet, in the airflow, for 3–5 minutes until the numbers stop changing. Don't touch the sensor tip and don't breathe on it.
  4. Take a photo showing the meter's screen with the FA duct behind it. Your phone records the date and time.
  5. Write down the temperature, humidity and dewpoint, plus the date and time. If you can, repeat early in the morning (around 7 am), when it's usually worst.
No access hatch? Then you can't reach the FA duct yourself. Skip this measurement, photograph the water damage, and ask the management company to measure the fresh air at the FA duct in your void. It's their system, and their engineers have the instruments. In Letter 1, delete the "What I measured" paragraph.

B. The ceiling void (if you have an access hatch)

  1. Open the hatch and use your phone torch to look at the pipe insulation. Photograph any droplets, wet patches or stains.
  2. Place the meter or sensor inside the void, lying on the ceiling board away from the hatch opening, and close the hatch. Leave it for at least 30 minutes, or overnight if it's an app sensor.
  3. Read the temperature, humidity and dewpoint. Note whether your air conditioning was on or off.

7Wait, chase, and handle the visit

  • Day 0
    Measure and send Letter 1
    Keep the sent email; it is your proof of notice.
  • After 2 working days
    No complaint number? Send Letter 2
    Management companies are expected to reply to written complaints within two working days.
  • When a technician comes
    Insist on a real measurement, then send Letter 3
    Write down what was measured and said, and confirm it by email the same day.
  • Closed without a fix, or no reply
    Send Letter 4 (objection) and go to sections 8 and 9
    A complaint closed as "resolved" while water still drips is the strongest evidence you will get.
  • Day 14
    Not fixed? Owners Committee and RERA
    Do both in parallel. If there's mould, add Dubai Municipality.

When the technician visits, make sure that:

  • your air conditioning has been off for a few hours before the visit, ideally overnight. With it running, the condensation hides and they'll report "no active condensation";
  • they measure temperature and humidity at the FA duct outlet in your void, not just look into the ceiling;
  • you get their name, the readings and the next step, with a date.

Don't accept "just run your air conditioning at 24–25 °C". It doesn't fix the cause, it moves the building's running costs onto you, and it fails the moment you travel.

9File with RERA on the website

RERA (the Real Estate Regulatory Agency, part of the Dubai Land Department) supervises management companies. Owners can file directly once the management company has been told in writing and has not replied properly within two working days.

Before you start, have ready: your UAE PASS login on your phone; your sent Letter 1 and any replies, saved as PDF; your photos; and a list of affected units (below). You can upload up to 10 files, each under 2 MB, as .jpg, .png or .pdf. Phone photos are often bigger than 2 MB, so shrink them or combine several into one PDF.
  1. Go to dubailand.gov.ae and click My DLD at the top right. Log in with UAE PASS.
  2. Open My Portfolio, then Owners Services (JOP), and choose your unit.
  3. Choose Complaints.
  4. Category: Management Performance.
    Sub category: Deterioration in the level of services provided in the property.
  5. Paste the text below into Description. The box takes at most 2,000 characters; the counter under the text shows the length.
  6. Attach your files under Attached Documents and submit. Save the reference number, and email it to the Owners Committee.

10If you have mould: Dubai Municipality

Mould from water ingress is a health issue, and that falls under Dubai Municipality. A Municipality inspector's report carries weight with RERA too.

  1. Photograph the mould with dates, close-up and wide, once a week. Growth over time is strong evidence.
  2. Report it by calling 800 900 or through dm.gov.ae/contact. Say: "mould and water ingress from the building's chilled water and fresh air systems in a residential apartment; I am requesting an inspection." Give your complaint number.
  3. Add the Municipality reference to your RERA complaint.
If you can, leave the mould in place until someone has inspected it, so there is no dispute about what was there. But if it's in a bedroom or anyone in your home has breathing problems, put health first: photograph it, then have it cleaned.

11What to do while you wait

  • Run your air conditioning cooler and slower: 20–22 °C with the fan on low. A colder coil and slower air pull more water out of the air. 24–25 °C on high mostly cools without drying.
  • A small dehumidifier in the worst room, set to 50%, helps a lot and costs far less to run than the air conditioning.
  • Put a tray or towel under any drip and check it daily. Keep photos.
  • Don't pay for new insulation or ceiling repairs yet. Insulation that worked for years isn't the cause, and repairs will get wet again until the air is fixed.
  • Keep your electricity bills. If you've been running the air conditioning extra to stop drips, that cost may matter later.
